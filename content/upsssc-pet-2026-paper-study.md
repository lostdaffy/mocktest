## 1. What the paper looks like

- 100 questions, 1 mark each, 2 hours. Questions sit in fixed blocks, always in this order.
- **Every question has 5 options: (A)–(D) plus (E) "उपर्युक्त में से कोई नहीं / None of the above".**
- Language: every question is printed Hindi first, then the same in English – except the
  English block (English only) and the Hindi blocks (Hindi only).
- Questions are short and direct: one line, one fact. Only one question in 100 used a
  "which pairs are correctly matched (I–IV)" format. Three asked "which is NOT …" with *not* in bold.

### How the data-interpretation sets are built (20 marks – the biggest single block)
- 4 sets × 5 questions: table, table, pie pair, double bar. Data has round numbers and a
  given total; the student never needs a calculator.
- Each set asks one of each: % increase between two columns; ratio of a combined pair to
  another combined pair; % share of the total; difference of two averages or two groups;
  and one **"what if" change** (X units moved from one item to another / one value up 20% /
  a target of 50% – find the new ratio or the shortfall).
- Answers come out as clean ratios (19:29, 16:15), fractions over the same denominator
  (options like 188/425, 222/425 …), mixed-number percentages (6⅔ %) or exact counts.
- Wrong options are the result of a common slip: wrong pair of rows, wrong base for the %,
  forgetting to change both sides in the "what if".

### Level
- Static GK, Geography, Science, Polity, Economy: easy-to-medium, textbook one-liners.
- History & National Movement: the hardest part – one level below the headline fact.
- Current affairs: 10 marks, specific (names, themes, editions), last 12–18 months.
- Maths and Reasoning: one-step or two-step, no lengthy calculation.
- Hindi passage and English: easy if read; options differ by one idea.
- Rough mix: 40 % easy, 45 % medium, 15 % hard.

#### G4 – Subject: *Maths* – Title: "PET 2026 real paper – maths and DI"
```
Real paper: 20 data-interpretation questions in 4 sets of 5 (table, table, pie chart pair with given totals, double bar graph) and only 5 arithmetic questions. DI data: 5-7 items, two columns or two series, round numbers, a given total. In every set ask: % increase between two columns; ratio of a combined pair to another combined pair; % or fraction of the total (options as fractions over the same denominator); difference of two averages; and one "what if" (some units moved from one item to another, one value up or down by a %, a target % to reach) asking the new ratio, difference or shortfall. Answers come out as clean ratios, exact counts or mixed-number percentages like 6 2/3%. Arithmetic: algebraic identity value, number of factors, largest perfect square up to N, successive % change over two years, missing value from a given average. Wrong options come from a common slip: wrong rows, wrong base for the %, changing only one side in the "what if".
```
